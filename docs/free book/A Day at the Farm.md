# A Day at the Farm

*Nuvo & the Wonder Crew: A Day at the Farm*. Each page is one interactive scene, with **one or two short sentences and three to five things to tap**.

The story should teach **animal names and sounds**, with a gentle message: **we care for animals by noticing what they need.**

The adventure begins with a question: **“What makes a happy farm?”** Nuvo and his friends discover the answer together.

| Page | Story and sample narration | Interactive discoveries |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Welcome to the Farm** | “The farm is waking up. What can we hear?” A farmer welcomes the crew. | Barn door creaks, birds chirp, distant cow moos |
| **2. Hello, Cow!** | “Moo! A cow stands beside her calf. They stay close together.” | Cow, calf, swishing tail |
| **3. Breakfast for the Sheep** | “The sheep are eating grass. Pip watches quietly beside Nuvo.” | Sheep baa, grass rustles, Pip sits |
| **4. A Busy Chicken** | “Cluck, cluck! A hen scratches the ground. Her chicks follow behind.” | Hen, peeping chicks, scratching feet |
| **5. Down by the Pond** | “The ducks paddle across the pond. Mimi watches from the path.” | Quacking ducks, ripples, rustling reeds |
| **6. A Gentle Hello** | “The farmer shows Lina how to greet the horse. Lina waits while the horse comes closer.” | Horse neighs, ears twitch, hoof taps |
| **7. What Do Animals Need?** | “Milo notices the water trough. Animals need fresh water, too!” | Water pours, cow drinks, bucket makes a soft clunk |
| **8. Here Comes the Rain** | “Drip, drop! The farmer opens the shelter. The animals have a dry place to rest.” | Raindrops, opening door, sheep stepping inside |
| **9. Something Is Peeping** | “Peep, peep! A chick is beside the wrong fence. Its hen is calling nearby.” | Chick peeps, hen clucks, friends listen |
| **10. Let’s Give It Space** | “Everyone steps back. The farmer opens the little gate, and the chick follows its hen.” | Gate opens, chick walks, family reunites |
| **11. A Happy Farm** | “Food, water, shelter—and gentle care. Now the crew knows what animals need.” | Revisit familiar animals and their sounds |
| **12. Goodbye for Now** | “‘Thank you, farm!’ says Nuvo. ‘What did you discover today?’” | Choose a favourite animal; optional “Find it” replay |

**The small chick adventure gives the book a story**, so it feels like more than a collection of animal facts. The children solve the problem by listening, asking an adult, and giving the animal space. Pip and Mimi help notice the sound without chasing the chick.

For your youngest readers, keep the main narration simple. Put extra vocabulary into optional taps: **cow → calf**, **sheep → lamb**, **hen → chick**. Children can enjoy the story without needing to remember every word.

I would also keep these rules throughout:

- **No feeding or handling animals without the farmer’s guidance.**
- **No quizzes that block the next page.** “Find the duck” is optional play.
- **Only one voice at a time.** Story narration pauses when a child explores.
- **A reassuring ending:** the chick rejoins its hen, and everyone leaves calmly.

Since this is your **free launch book**, make all 12 story pages available. It should feel like a complete adventure that earns parents’ trust and introduces the crew.

# Note

**NuvoKids Books is primarily for ages 2–5**, with different ways to enjoy each book:

| Age | Best experience |
|---|---|
| **2–3** | Explore with a parent: tap pictures, hear names and sounds, repeat familiar words |
| **3–4** | Follow short narrated stories, answer simple “Find it” prompts, notice actions |
| **4–5** | Predict what happens next, learn new vocabulary, discuss feelings and ideas |

*A Day at the Farm* fits this full range: younger children enjoy the animals and sounds; older children follow the chick’s reunion and learn about animal care.

For marketing, use: **“Interactive stories and sound books for ages 2–5.”**

Narration means children **do not need to read independently**. Future books can have their own age labels if you expand to ages 5–7.
